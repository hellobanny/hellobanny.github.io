2What we do not do

  • We do not require an account, and we do not collect a phone number, email address or password for sign-in.
  • We do not build user profiles, run advertising, or include any advertising SDK.
  • We do not integrate third-party analytics, crash reporting, social login or data-mining SDKs. The current version does not even enable first-party event tracking.
  • We do not sell, rent or trade your personal information.
  • We do not track your location in the background, read photos you did not select or authorize, or use contacts, photos or location for advertising or model training.

To be clear: to make a feature work, the app may read or modify some information on your device, such as contact fields, camera frames or location coordinates. That happens only after you open the relevant feature. Details follow.

3.1 Camera

Camera access is requested only when you enter a feature that needs the camera. Each feature handles frames differently:

  • Balcony Sunlight uses the AR camera view to help you outline a balcony or window. When generating a report you may take an optional site photo; only if you keep it is the photo written to the app's local report folder, where it can be viewed on device or shared by you.
  • AR Compass overlays the live camera view on a direction scale. It does not take, save or upload photos.
  • Camera Level shows a preview with horizontal and vertical guides. It does not take or save photos.
  • Mirror in the Dark shows the front camera as a mirror, with white areas above and below as fill light. It does not take or save photos. It raises screen brightness to maximum while open and restores it when you leave.
  • Party Draw can photograph a participant to create a draw token. The photo lives only in memory for that game and is discarded when you leave the feature; it is not written to Photos or to a local file.

3.2 Location (While Using)

The app requests “While Using the App” location only. It does not request background location, continuously track you, or store a movement history. Location is used to:

  • Balcony Sunlight: compute the solar path, daylight duration and winter-solstice indicators from latitude and longitude. You can also type coordinates manually and use the feature without granting location.
  • AR Compass: convert magnetic north to true north, and look up landmarks around you for “Nearby Places”.

Your coordinates are not sent to our servers. Two kinds of requests go to Apple's map services: when you search for nearby places, the current coordinate and search radius are sent to Apple Maps; when Balcony Sunlight cannot obtain a place name from the system, it sends the coordinate to Apple for a one-time reverse geocode to obtain a city or district name. Apple and its map data providers handle those requests under Apple's own privacy policy.

3.3 Contacts (Contact Quick Search)

This feature reads and modifies contacts so that typing zsf in Spotlight or Contacts can find 张三丰. The scope is limited:

  • Reading: after you tap “Write initials” and grant access, the app reads contact names (family name, given name, nickname, phonetic name), organization name, the first phone number and the first email address. Phone and email are used only to filter by the “has a phone or email” scope and to show the last digits; they are not written anywhere.
  • Writing: the pinyin initials computed from a Chinese name (for example zsf) are written into the field you choose — Phonetic Given Name or Nickname. The app does not create contacts, delete contacts, or change phone numbers, email addresses, organization names or any other field.
  • Local ledger: for each modified contact, the app stores the contact identifier, field name, original value and written value so that “Restore originals and turn off” can put things back exactly. The ledger stays in the app's local data container and is not uploaded.
  • System indexing and sync: because the app writes to contact fields themselves, iOS Contacts and Spotlight index that content on device. If you enable iCloud Contacts, those fields sync to your other devices; sharing a contact card also includes them. The app does not create its own cloud index.
  • Withdrawal and cleanup: “Restore originals and turn off” writes the stored originals back and clears the ledger. “Clean up leftover initials” does not rely on the ledger; it recomputes initials from names and removes them. Before uninstalling the app, restore first — otherwise the ledger is deleted with the app and written initials must be handled manually.
  • Legacy index: an earlier version created a separate Spotlight index. The current version removes those legacy entries when enabling or restoring, and does not create new ones.
Please note: contacts are sensitive personal information. Writing initials is entirely your choice, and denying contacts access does not affect other features. Once written, the initials appear in the field you selected and may leave the device through iCloud sync or a shared contact card. That is how contact sync works; it is not an upload by the app.

3.4 Motion and orientation sensors

The app reads gravity and device attitude through Core Motion to tell whether the phone is level and which way it faces. This supports the level guides, compass direction and AR measurement. The data is used on device only; it is not written to Health and is not uploaded.

3.5 Photos you select

Party Draw can use a photo from your library as a draw-token background. It uses the iOS system photo picker: the app receives only the single image you tap, cannot browse or read other photos, and does not request full photo-library access for this feature. The image is used only in that game and is not saved or uploaded.

The optional site photo in Balcony Sunlight likewise comes from a single image you take or select. The Pixel Ruler feature is not included in the current release; if it is enabled later, imported screenshots will also be processed only on device.

3.6 Purchases and entitlements

Lifetime Unlock (non-consumable) and the 72-Hour Pass (consumable) are handled through Apple StoreKit. We do not receive your card number, billing address or Apple ID password; the app reads only the product ID, transaction ID, purchase or revocation status and expiry.

Lifetime access follows Apple's verified transaction result. The 72-hour pass expiry is stored in the local Keychain so the window can continue while active. Keychain data may migrate with a system backup, but restoration after a device change or reinstall is not guaranteed, as stated on the purchase screen.

3.7 Local data and settings

Feature data lives in the app sandbox under Application Support/Features/<feature ID>. It includes daylight reports, thumbnails and site photos, the contacts ledger, and per-feature settings such as frame position, draw-music preference and measured device weight. Settings use a separate UserDefaults suite per feature and do not touch the global domain.

Retention: daylight reports and site photos are kept until you delete the report or uninstall the app; the contacts ledger is kept until you restore or uninstall; feature settings are kept until you change them or uninstall; the 72-hour pass expiry stays in the Keychain until it expires, is replaced by a new transaction, or is cleared by the system. We hold no server copy, so there is nothing extra for us to retain or delete.

The app does not write feature data to iCloud Documents or CloudKit. If you enable iCloud device backup, the app sandbox may be included in Apple's encrypted system backup, which Apple handles. Uninstalling the app deletes sandbox data; the pass record in the Keychain may survive a reinstall, which is system behavior.

4Third parties and disclosures

We do not disclose your personal information to third parties except in these cases:

  1. Apple system services: Apple Maps and geocoding, StoreKit purchases, iCloud Contacts sync and device backup, as needed to provide the features described above.
  2. Legal requirements: where required by law, or by a judicial or administrative authority following lawful procedure.
  3. Vital interests: where necessary to protect your or another person's life, property or other major interests.

We do not sell personal information. We do not transfer personal information across borders ourselves; Apple services such as Maps and StoreKit may process data at facilities inside or outside your region under Apple's privacy policy. The app contains no third-party advertising, analytics or data-broker SDK.

5Data security

  • Transmission: the app does not transmit data to our servers. Requests to Apple services are made by system frameworks over encrypted connections.
  • Storage: feature data sits in the iOS app sandbox and is subject to system data protection. Purchase credentials are stored in the Keychain.
  • Permissions: we follow least privilege and request access only when you use a feature. Denying a permission affects that feature only; you can turn permissions off at any time in iOS Settings → Oddkit.
  • Support: the app is maintained by an individual developer and there is no support backend that can view your feature data. When asking for help, share only screenshots or descriptions you are comfortable making public — please do not send contacts, original reports or other sensitive files.

6Your rights

Under the Personal Information Protection Law of the People's Republic of China and other applicable laws, you may access, correct, withdraw consent to and delete your personal information. Because the data is mainly on your device, most actions are direct:

  • Access and correction: contact fields can be viewed and edited in the system Contacts app; daylight reports can be viewed in the feature; settings can be adjusted in the relevant feature.
  • Withdraw consent: turn off Camera, Location, Contacts and Motion & Fitness in iOS Settings → Oddkit, or use “Restore originals and turn off” inside the feature.
  • Deletion: delete individual daylight reports in the feature, or delete the app to clear sandbox data. We hold no copy of your data, so no separate server-side deletion request is needed.
  • Questions and complaints: email hellobanny@gmail.com and we will reply within 15 business days.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights under local law. We will honor valid requests that apply to the data we actually control, which is limited to the information described in this policy.

7Children

Oddkit is a general-purpose utility, is not specifically designed for children, does not collect children's personal information, and does not ask for age. If a guardian finds that a child has used a permission-protected feature without consent, they can turn the permission off in system settings or delete the app, and contact us at the email above.

8Policy updates

When features or data practices change, we update this policy and publish the new version in the app and on the website together, with the last-updated date at the top. For material changes — such as adding uploads, a third-party SDK, a new permission or a new purpose — we will notify you in the app or in release notes before they take effect, and will ask for consent again where required.
